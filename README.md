# GTA 7

A fan-made open-world crime game in the browser, in the spirit of the GTA
games, for you and up to three friends. Steal cars, outrun the police, walk
into buildings and take jobs from the people who run the city of San Cielo,
then take the highway out of town and see the rest of the island, from
morning till night and round again. The story, places, characters and map
are all original. (GTA 7 isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Rockstar Games.)

You're Isaiah "Zay" Carter from the Flats: twenty-three, broke, with a car
and a best friend, Dre, who is always one bad idea from trouble. The game
opens at home at eight in the morning. Dre's in your living room eating your
cereal, and he's found the two of you some work. The people with jobs:

- **Dre**, who brings you the first one and owes money to the wrong people.
- **Ray Okafor**, who sells cars in Eastgate and buys back the ones people
  stopped paying for.
- **Lena Voss**, who runs the freight yards at the Docks and everything that
  goes through them.
- **Vince Moreno**, who robbed armoured trucks for twenty years and retired to
  a big house in Palm Ridge, mostly.

Each of them waits in their room (Dre in your living room, the others in
their offices): walk in through the door and step into the ring in front of
them, and the job starts with a cutscene. There are eight jobs:

1. **Easy Money** (Dre): Dre rides along to Pelican Beach to repossess a car
   for Okafor.
2. **Alarm Bells** (Okafor): steal a dentist's Kestrel in Palm Ridge, lose
   the police (or get a respray at Spray-N-Go) and bring it in.
3. **Bad Company** (Dre): Dre owes Teddy Brill two grand. A gunfight at the
   Pool Hall on the Strip.
4. **The Driver** (Voss): wait outside the Diamond Exchange downtown, then
   get the crew to Pier 9 with three stars on you.
5. **Street Kings** (Dre): a fifteen-checkpoint street race from Starlight
   through Downtown and Eastgate.
6. **Hot Cargo** (Voss): take a truck from San Cielo International and lose
   the police before you get back to the Docks.
7. **The Score** (Vince): stop an armoured truck, grab the cash bags and lose
   four stars.
8. **Paid in Full** (Vince): Voss has taken Dre. A shootout at the container
   terminal, and you get him home.

Between jobs the city is yours. Twenty hidden packages are scattered round
town and the island ($500 each, and a bonus for finding them all). Guns and
armour lie about, or you can buy them over the counter inside Freedom Arms.
A meal at Sal's Diner puts you back to full health, and Spray-N-Go will
respray your car and make the police forget you were ever in it. A whole day
takes 48 minutes, and the street lights and lit windows come on at dusk.

## San Cielo

The city is a kilometre across with an uneven, stepped edge. Its districts:

- **Pelican Beach**, the boardwalk and sand along the west side, with the
  pier and its café.
- **Palm Ridge** in the north-west: palm-lined streets, big modern houses
  with pools, and the Moreno Estate.
- **Starlight**, the entertainment district: the Starlight Theatre, the
  Grand Hotel, clubs, cinemas and the Strip.
- **Downtown**, with the towers, City Hall's plaza, the Diamond Exchange and
  the bank. **Grand Park** and **Mercy Hill**, with the hospital, are north
  of it.
- **Mirror Hills** and **Eastgate** in the east, with Okafor's Motors.
- **The Flats**, where you live: small houses, the police station and the
  rail yard.
- **Mill End** and **The Docks** in the south-east: warehouses, Spray-N-Go,
  Voss Freight and the container terminal, with **the Harbour** and its
  cranes along the south shore.

## The island

San Cielo is on the south coast of an island about 2 km across and 3 km from
end to end. It's laid out the way GTA V's is (a city in the south, hills,
desert, a lake and a big mountain to the north), but every bit of it was
drawn from scratch:

- **San Cielo International**, the airport, out on the peninsula south-west
  of town.
- **The Starlight Hills** north of town, with the big white SAN CIELO sign,
  the villas and the winding Hill Road. **Eastgate Shore** runs up the east
  coast.
- **The Redwater Desert** in the middle of the island, with an airstrip, and
  **Lake Corrigan** north of it. **Dust Creek** (a bar, a motel, a gas
  station and trailers) is where the freeway meets the Desert Road.
- **Mount Calder** and a wind farm in the east. **Mount Harlow**, the
  island's highest point (with a mast on top), is in the north-west, in the
  pine forests round **Port Kerry** at the northern tip.

The **Coast Highway** runs up the west coast, the **Eastern Freeway** up the
middle-east, the **Shore Road** up the east side, the **Desert Road** across
the middle and the **Hill Road** over the hills. That's about 10 km of road
in all, with traffic on it. Press **M** for the map: scroll (or + and -) to
zoom, and drag (or use the arrow keys) to move it about.

**Play it:** <https://nickypeakin.github.io/nightsecurity/>. It works in
Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer with a keyboard and mouse. Headphones
help.

## How it plays

- **Jobs.** The big letters on the radar (D, O, V, M) are the people with
  work. Go in through their door and step into the glowing ring in front of
  them to start a job (you can't have any stars).
  The objective is at the bottom of the screen, and what you need is on the
  radar: yellow for where to go, blue for a car or a friend, red for a
  target, green for something to pick up. If Dre's along and he goes down,
  the job's failed. Fail one and you can walk back in and try again.
- **Stars.** Crimes make the police want you: stealing cars (especially
  theirs), hitting or running people over, gunfire, killing. Stars bring
  police cars, then officers on foot, and from two stars they shoot. Get out
  of every cop's sight long enough and the stars go (the more stars, the
  longer it takes), or drive into Spray-N-Go for $100. Let an officer get
  hold of you and you're **busted**: you lose your guns and $150. Die and
  you're **wasted**, and the hospital charges $100.
- **Buildings.** Walk into a glowing door to go inside (you can't with the
  police after you): your place, Freedom Arms, Sal's Diner, Okafor's Motors,
  Voss Freight and the Moreno Estate. Nobody fights indoors.
- **Your place and your car.** Your Kestrel is parked by your house (the
  blue arrow on the radar). Wreck it and a new one turns up there. Walk up to
  your bed to save. The game also saves when you pass a job or find a
  package, and **Continue** on the main menu picks up where you left off.

## Playing with friends

- **Alone:** **Story Mode**, then **New Game** (or **Continue**).
- **With friends:** in **Online**, one person clicks **Host a Game** and sends the invite
  link from the lobby (the **Copy** button). Friends open the link and press
  **Join the Game**, or open the game and type the 5-letter code. The host
  clicks **Start** once everyone's there.

Everyone shares the jobs (any of you can do any step, and everyone gets
paid), but each of you has your own money, guns and wanted level. Climb into
a friend's car with F to ride along and shoot out of the window.

There's no game server. The host's browser runs the city, and friends
connect straight to it over WebRTC. The free [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com)
service is only used to find each other by lobby code. The host has to keep
the game open while everyone plays. If friends can't get in, have everyone
press **Test Connection** on the main menu. Some school and work networks
block these connections, and switching either player to a different network
(a phone hotspot works) usually fixes it.

## Publishing to GitHub Pages

`.github/workflows/pages.yml` tests the game, builds it and publishes it on
every push to the default branch. (If Pages isn't switched on yet: in the
repository go to **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source** and
pick **GitHub Actions**.)

## Running it locally

Needs Node.js 20 or newer.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev      # builds dist/ and serves it at http://localhost:8080
npm test         # plays every job headless, and checks the city's layout
```

## How it's built

Plain JavaScript modules and [three.js](https://threejs.org). Every model,
texture and sound is generated in code, so there are no asset files.

The city's plan is a small table in `shared/plan.js`: streets run along
about two dozen north-south and east-west lines (closer together downtown), each
square between them is a letter for what's there (beach, mansions,
Starlight, downtown, the park, the Flats, the docks and so on) or a dot
where the city stops, and a few blocks take up more than one square.
`shared/city.js` turns that into junctions with traffic lights, the blocks
and their buildings, parking spaces, street lamps, palms and props, and the
pavements' walking network. The host builds its collision from that, and
each client builds the 3D city from the same plan. The city is merged into a
few big meshes per 120 m square so it draws quickly, and the shop signs are
painted onto a few shared pictures. Hundreds of street lamps light the city
through one patched three.js shader that looks lamps up in a small texture
laid over the map.

The island is plain numbers too (`shared/world.js`): a coastline, a height
for every 5 m worked out from a few hills and mountains, noise, beaches and
cliffs, the lake's hollow and the city's flat ground, and the highways as
smooth curves that the land is cut and banked to fit, never steeper than 1
in 9. The highways join the city's road graph as chains of junctions, so the
traffic, the police and the jobs' drivers use them like any street. The
land is drawn a 320 m square at a time (the far squares with a quarter of
the triangles, and a skirt so they never show a crack), with the trees and
rocks merged a square at a time too. From far off, each square of town is a
single mesh of plain boxes whose windows still light up at night.

The rooms you can walk into are laid out at sea, off the south-east coast,
where nobody will see them. A door takes you there (with a quick fade), and
while you're inside only that room is drawn, lit by the same light-grid
shader as the streets (its ceiling lights are always on). The sun, the sky
and the haze follow a 24-hour clock that starts at 8 AM. Cars use one arcade
physics model (`shared/car.js`) that your own browser runs for the car you
drive and the host runs for all the traffic. The people are procedural
bodies, turned into skinned meshes so a crowd costs only a few draw calls.

who's hit, who's wanted, how each job is going). Each client drives its own
character or car, says what it shoots at, and draws the rest from the host's
snapshots, ten a second, a little behind so everything moves smoothly.

### Where it came from

This repository started out as **Night Security**, a co-op horror game set
in a shopping mall. None of that is left now. What was kept is the engine:
the networking and lobby, the procedural people, the light-grid shader,
post-processing, synthesised sound and spoken voices, input, the build and
the Pages workflow. Most of that came from the other games on this account,
especially [Cinder Peak](https://github.com/nickypeakin/fears-to-fathom), and
the networking core is the one [backrooms](https://github.com/nickypeakin/backrooms)
uses. The city, the island, the cars, the traffic, the police, the guns, the
story and the jobs are all new.
